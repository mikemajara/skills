# Long-form writing

Use for essays, articles, and writing published under a personal byline.
Long-form work needs one sustained idea, not announcement structure stretched
across more paragraphs.

## Editorial pass

1. Identify the argument or observation the piece adds. A topic alone is not a
   point.
2. Open with the tension, claim, or concrete observation that creates a reason
   to continue.
3. Arrange sections by the development of the idea, not by the order in which
   the author discovered it.
4. Give each section one job. Remove sections that repeat the premise without
   advancing it.
5. Use examples as evidence. Explain what each example demonstrates rather
   than expecting it to carry the argument by itself.
6. Keep qualifications that make the claim accurate, but place them beside the
   claim they qualify.
7. End when the argument has landed. A summary or call to action is optional,
   not automatic.

## Protect the piece

Do not flatten a personal argument into generic advice. Preserve useful
specificity, uncertainty, and changes of mind. Cut throat-clearing and repeated
context before cutting the details that make the idea credible.
