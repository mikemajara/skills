# Announcements

Use for internal team updates, launches, share-outs, and work that needs
visibility. The reader should quickly understand what changed, why it matters,
and what happens next.

## Common failure modes

1. **Disclaimer opener:** a caveat or apology appears before the outcome.
2. **Sequential narration:** the post follows the order of the work rather than
   the order of reader value.
3. **Buried headline:** what changed appears after background or implementation.
4. **Facts before framing:** details arrive before the reader knows why they
   matter.
5. **Hidden ownership:** passive voice makes the contribution unclear.
6. **Dense benefits:** several benefits are buried in one sentence.
7. **Context without a close:** the post ends without access, ownership, or a
   useful next step.

## Structure

1. Open with what exists or changed and why the audience should care.
2. Name the work and its owner plainly when ownership is relevant.
3. Put outcomes and audience benefits before implementation details.
4. Use a numbered list when there are two or more distinct benefits or changes.
5. Add one short piece of timely context only when it proves relevance.
6. Close with the appropriate next step: try it, review it, request access,
   respond, or contact the owner. Omit a forced call to action when none exists.

Stay direct and factual. Avoid launch clichés, inflated claims, defensive tool
comparisons, and backstory that matters only to the author.
