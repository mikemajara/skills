# Editorial workflow

Use this workflow for articles and website copy that need coordinated work from a brief to a verified publication. Keep the editorial task in the project's existing tracker and the working article in the chosen content source. Link them; do not create a second backlog.

## Workflow

1. **Brief.** Confirm the audience, search intent when relevant, angle, brand voice, sources or research needs, call to action, scope, destination, and approver. Ask only for missing details that would materially change the piece.
2. **Research.** Gather evidence for factual claims. Prefer primary sources for niche, current, or high-stakes claims. Record links beside claims or in the draft's source notes. Label hypothetical examples clearly. Never invent clients, results, volumes, quotations, or firsthand experience.
3. **Draft.** Write for the audience and destination, following the agreed brief and voice. Add relevant SEO metadata, internal links, or social preview text when they serve the destination.
4. **Editorial review.** Review a specific draft version for accuracy, sourcing, audience fit, structure, brand consistency, links, and the brief's requirements. The author may self-check, but must not describe that as an independent review. Keep review proportional: a headline or small copy change needs a focused check, not a multi-person process.
5. **Approval.** Present the exact version, destination, and any material publication details to the designated approver. Approval applies only to that version and destination. Do not publish, send, or submit content without the required authorization. Honor explicit prior authorization within its stated scope.
6. **Publication check.** After authorized publication, inspect the live destination. Record its URL, status, date, and any needed corrections in the canonical task. A draft being ready or approved does not mean it is published.

Keep these states distinct: **draft-ready**, **approved**, and **published**. If substantive edits happen after review or approval, reopen only the affected checks and obtain approval for the changed version before publication.

Use the software workflow for code, templates, or metadata changes that are implementation work; editorial signoff does not replace software QA.
