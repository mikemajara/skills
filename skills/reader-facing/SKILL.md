# Reader-facing

Write what the reader needs to know, not what the assistant needs to demonstrate
it understood. Instructions for the writer are not automatically information
for the reader.

## Rules

1. Follow drafting constraints silently. Do not turn "leave X out" into a
   sentence announcing that X is excluded.
2. Keep process notes, editorial rationale, and acknowledgments outside the
   finished content. Surface them separately only when useful; do not add a
   routine explanation of what you changed.
3. Give each field its own job. In structured forms, comments add evidence or
   explanation rather than restating the adjacent response label. A form's
   comment field is reader-facing content, not an editorial discussion thread.
4. Open with the subject itself. Add a purpose introduction such as "This
   document outlines…" only when it genuinely helps the reader navigate or
   understand the content.
5. Mention exclusions when explicitly requested or necessary to prevent a
   material misunderstanding, not merely because they appeared in the brief.
   Preserve substantive limitations, uncertainty, and negative facts that
   change the meaning. Do not hide missing evidence to make prose cleaner.
6. Preserve the supplied facts and requirements while removing commentary.
   Do not invent completion conditions or weaken a requirement into a capability:
   "Recovery requires an administrator" is not the same as "An administrator
   can restore files."

## Examples

- Response: "Not applicable." Comment: "The service does not store payment-card
  data." Do not append "Therefore, this requirement is not applicable."
- Brief: "Explain onboarding; leave billing out." Start with the onboarding
  steps, not "This guide covers onboarding but excludes billing."
- Necessary limitation: "Recovery is available for 30 days after deletion."
  Keep it: the reader needs that boundary to act correctly.

## Final check

Ask of each sentence: "Does this inform the reader, or just show that I followed
an instruction?" Remove the latter. Check neighboring fields and the existing
thread before repeating context.

This skill owns the boundary between drafting context and finished content,
not word count or voice. Combine it with other writing and collaboration skills
without removing requested rationale, useful discussion, or required attribution.
