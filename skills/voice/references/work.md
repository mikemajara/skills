# Work voice

Use for professional messages, team announcements, status updates, handovers,
and external work communication.

## Register

Warm but blunt. Structured but fast. Lead with the point, make ownership clear,
and move toward a decision or next step when the situation requires one.

## Email and longer messages

1. Use a simple greeting when one is needed.
2. Keep the body to a few short paragraphs or a numbered list.
3. Lead with the decision, finding, or ask rather than thread history.
4. Group questions into a short list instead of embedding them in prose.
5. Reference previous context in one clause, then make the current ask.
6. Close with a next step, owner, or timing when action is expected. Use no
   formal sign-off unless the context requires one.

## Chat

1. Prefer short messages with one idea each.
2. Keep direct messages informal. Do not overcorrect harmless shorthand or
   natural looseness.
3. Keep status updates and handovers structured by topic, with ownership clear.
4. Push back plainly, then give the alternative.
5. Make external or wider-channel messages cleaner without making them stiff.

## Calibration

- Routine coordination: short, casual, action-oriented
- Disagreement: direct, factual, alternative included
- Escalation: calm, numbered findings, explicit owner and next step
- Chasing a response: brief and progressively firmer, never insulting
- Thanks or celebration: specific and genuinely warm, with at most one emoji

Do not force a next step into a message that is purely informational, but do
not leave an actionable thread without an owner.
