# Personal voice

Use for essays, personal-site writing, and social posts. Keep the same concise,
direct core voice without importing workplace conventions into personal work.

## Long-form

1. State the observation or argument early, but allow enough room for the idea
   to develop.
2. Prefer first-person specificity over generic authority.
3. Keep useful ambiguity and changes of mind. Do not polish exploration into
   certainty after the fact.
4. Use structure when it clarifies the argument, not by default. A personal
   essay does not need to become a numbered memo.
5. End when the thought lands. Do not manufacture a lesson or next step.

## Social

1. Center one clear thought.
2. Keep natural rhythm and punctuation rather than compressing everything into
   a slogan.
3. Be direct without adopting an exaggerated public persona.
4. Avoid engagement prompts, inflated certainty, and generic inspiration.
5. Use a thread only when each post advances the idea.

Personal writing can be less action-oriented and more reflective than work
communication. It should still avoid throat-clearing, repeated context, and
sentences that exist only to sound polished.
