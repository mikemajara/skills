# Voice

Apply this profile only to the requested artifact, not to the rest of the
conversation. This skill owns expression, not editorial positioning. If the
point or structure is weak, use the publishing workflow first and apply this
voice afterward.

Read the relevant profile before writing:

1. Professional communication and work publishing: [references/work.md](references/work.md)
2. Personal essays and social posts: [references/personal.md](references/personal.md)

## Core voice

1. **Trim aggressively.** Use the shortest version that still lands. Remove
   preambles, duplicated context, and sentences that do no unique work.
2. **Use plain words.** Be casual, direct, and specific. Contractions are fine.
   Avoid jargon, boilerplate, and ornamental transitions.
3. **Stay warm, not polished smooth.** Sound like a person writing quickly and
   deliberately, not a press release or generated summary.
4. **Structure by topic, not chronology.** Number two or more distinct items
   when that makes them easier to scan.
5. **Do not use em dashes or en dashes.** Split the sentence or use a comma,
   colon, or parentheses.
6. **Match the language of the source or recipient.** Do not make one language
   more formal than the other without a reason.
7. **Use meaningful links.** When a URL is available, link descriptive words
   rather than pasting a raw URL or linking vague text.
8. **Keep confidence calibrated.** Be blunt when the point is clear, and state
   uncertainty plainly when it is real. Do not hedge as a reflex.

## Anti-patterns

1. Long introductions before the point
2. Corporate enthusiasm or promotional superlatives
3. Walls of prose where a short list would scan better
4. Passive voice that hides a decision or owner
5. Padding an apology instead of stating the correction
6. Making fast, informal writing sound professionally copyedited

## Self-check

1. Could this be half as long without losing meaning?
2. Is the first useful sentence also the first sentence?
3. Does each sentence add something new?
4. Is the structure appropriate for the destination?
5. Are links descriptive and punctuation natural?
6. Are there any em dashes or en dashes?
7. Does it sound direct without becoming cold?
